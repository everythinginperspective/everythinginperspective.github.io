## The Mnemonic

**ROY G. BIV** is a name-based acronym representing the visible light spectrum in order:

- **R**ed
- **O**range
- **Y**ellow
- **G**reen
- **B**lue
- **I**ndigo
- **V**iolet

## How It Works

When light passes through a prism (or water droplets in rain), it splits into these seven distinct color bands. The order matters because it represents **increasing frequency and decreasing wavelength**.

Red has the longest wavelength; violet has the shortest.

## Memory Aid

Imagine **Roy G. Biv** as a person's name. "Roy" is memorable, and "G. Biv" sounds like a last name. Together, they encode all seven colors in sequence.

## Why This Order?

The spectrum is determined by **wavelength**:
- Red: ~700 nm (longest)
- Violet: ~400 nm (shortest)

The mnemonic preserves this natural ordering from longest to shortest wavelength.

## Real-World Application

Use this when:
- Explaining how prisms work
- Understanding light refraction
- Teaching color theory in physics
