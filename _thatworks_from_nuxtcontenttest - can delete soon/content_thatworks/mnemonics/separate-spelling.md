## The Mnemonic

**Separate** is frequently misspelled. Use this visual trick:

**sep-A-R-A-T-e**

There's a **RAT** in sep**ARATE**: **A-R-A**

## How It Works

The word contains the sequence A-R-A in the middle. If you remember "there's a RAT," you'll spell it correctly:
- Se**P**-**ARATE**
- The RAT (A-R-A-T) comes after the P

## Common Mistakes

People often write:
- "Seperate" (missing the second A)
- "Seperete" (rearranged letters)
- "Seprate" (missing both A's)

## Memory Enhancement

Visualize a rat running through the word: **S-e-p-[RAT]-e**. The visual of an actual rat helps lock in the spelling.

Alternatively: **"There's a RAT in sep**A**R**A**T**e"** — say this phrase out loud, and the rhythm helps memory.

## Similar Words

Other words with hidden animals or objects:
- "Stationery" (paper) vs. "Stationary" (not moving) - Remember the "E" in "stationEry" is for papEr
- "Privilege" - "There's **ILI** in priv**ILI**lege"

## Etymology

The word comes from Latin "separatus," meaning "parted" or "divided." The double A in the root emphasizes the idea of separation into distinct parts.
