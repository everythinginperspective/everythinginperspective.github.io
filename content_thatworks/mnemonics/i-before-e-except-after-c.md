## The Mnemonic

**"I before E, except after C"** is a classic English spelling rule:

- Write **IE** in most words: *believe*, *chief*, *relief*, *field*
- Write **EI** after C: *ceiling*, *receive*, *deceive*, *conceive*

## How It Works

The rule catches about 70% of IE/EI words correctly, making it statistically useful even if not perfect.

## Examples

**IE words (not after C):**
- believe, brief, chief, grief, niece, piece, thief, yield, shield

**EI words (after C):**
- ceiling, conceive, deceive, receipt, receive

## Important Exceptions

This rule is full of exceptions, which is why English spelling is notoriously tricky:

- **"Weird" exception**: "Weird," "seize," "either," "neither" violate the rule
- **"Science" exception**: Words like "science," "ancient," "sufficient" also break it
- **Vowel sounds matter**: The rule works better when you pronounce the vowel combination aloud

## Memory Enhancement

Add this phrase to the rule: **"I before E, except after C, or when sounding like 'ay' as in 'neighbor' and 'weigh.'"**

This catches more words correctly.

## Why This Rule Exists

English inherited these spelling patterns from Old French and Middle English. The pattern emerged from how the language evolved rather than pure phonetic logic.
