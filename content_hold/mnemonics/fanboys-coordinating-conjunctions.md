## The Mnemonic

**FANBOYS** lists all seven coordinating conjunctions in English:

- **F**or
- **A**nd
- **N**or
- **B**ut
- **O**r
- **Y**et
- **S**o

## How It Works

Coordinating conjunctions connect two independent clauses (complete sentences). When used, they're preceded by a comma.

Examples:
- "I want ice cream, **but** it's too cold." (contrast)
- "You can go left, **or** you can go right." (choice)
- "She studied hard, **so** she passed the test." (consequence)

## Memory Aid

**FANBOYS** sounds like a group name—memorable and easy to chant. Think of them as the seven "connectors" of equal-weight ideas.

## Each Conjunction's Role

- **For**: Cause/reason ("He stayed home, **for** he was sick")
- **And**: Addition ("I like pizza **and** pasta")
- **Nor**: Negative choice ("Neither here **nor** there")
- **But**: Contrast ("She tried, **but** failed")
- **Or**: Positive choice ("Tea **or** coffee?")
- **Yet**: Contrast with surprise ("Poor, **yet** happy")
- **So**: Consequence ("I was tired, **so** I left")

## Punctuation Rule

Always use a comma before a coordinating conjunction when connecting independent clauses. This rule is sometimes called the **comma splice rule**.
