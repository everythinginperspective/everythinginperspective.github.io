## The Mnemonic

French nouns are either **masculine** or **feminine**. Endings often reveal gender:

**Feminine patterns:**
- Words ending in **-E**: la table, la maison, la page (most common)
- Words ending in **-LE**: la bataille, la fille (battle, girl)
- Words ending in **-ETTE**: la cassette, la palette (cassette, palette)
- Words ending in **-TION**: la nation, la solution (nation, solution)
- Words ending in **-URE**: la nature, la culture (nature, culture)
- Words ending in **-EE**: la poupée, la journée (doll, day)

**Masculine patterns:**
- Words ending in **-EAU**: le bureau, le tableau, le château (office, picture, castle)
- Words ending in **-AGE**: le village, le garage, le voyage (village, garage, journey)
- Words ending in **-ATE**: le pirate, le dentate (pirate, dentate)
- Words ending in **-OIR**: le miroir, le devoir (mirror, homework)
- Words ending in **-ET**: le paquet, le complet (package, complete)

## How It Works

These patterns aren't 100% reliable, but they're statistically accurate:
- **~75% of French words ending in -E are feminine**
- **~75% of French words ending in -EAU are masculine**
- **~80% of French words ending in -TION are feminine**

## Memory Technique

**Create mental maps:**
- **Feminine (-E):** Picture a feminine figure, ending with the letter E
- **Masculine (-EAU):** Picture water (eau), which is beautiful/aesthetic (stereotypically associated with masculine articles le bel, le beaux)
- **-TION = -SION:** Almost always **feminine** — think of "action" (la action → l'action is feminine)

## Exception Categories

Some important exceptions to memorize:
- **-TION masculine:** le bastion, le nation (but "nation" is typically feminine: "la nation")
- **-E masculine:** le café, le lycée, le musée, le pedigree, le résumé (many borrowed/technical words)
- **-AGE feminine (rare):** l'image, la plage, la page, l'adolescence (a few notable exceptions)

## Practice Method

When learning new French words:
1. Notice the ending first
2. Check the article (le = masculine, la = feminine, l' = check context)
3. Log exceptions separately

## Real-World Usage

French articles are mandatory, so gender impacts:
- Article choice: **le** vs. **la** vs. **l'**
- Adjective agreement: **beau** (m) vs. **belle** (f)
- Past participles: **travaillé** (m) vs. **travaillée** (f)

Getting gender right affects comprehension and fluency significantly.
