## The Mnemonic

Spanish regular verbs in present tense follow a predictable pattern. Use this rhythm:

**"Yo soy, tú eres, él es"** (for the verb "ser" = to be)

Or more generally for **-AR** verbs:
**"Yo hablo, tú hablas, él habla"** (I speak, you speak, he speaks)

## How It Works

The pattern is:
- **Yo** (I): Base form + **-o**
- **Tú** (You informal): Base form + **-as** (for -AR verbs)
- **Él/Ella** (He/She): Base form + **-a**
- **Nosotros**: Base form + **-amos**
- **Vosotros**: Base form + **-áis**
- **Ellos**: Base form + **-an**

## Memory Technique

Chant the verb conjugation aloud with rhythm:
- "Ha-BLO, ha-BLAS, ha-BLA" (stress pattern helps memory)
- The **-O, -AS, -A** endings are the most important to lock in

## Subject Pronouns & Endings

Think of it as a **person-to-ending mapping**:
- 1st person singular (Yo): **-o**
- 2nd person singular (Tú): **-s** (adds to base)
- 3rd person singular (Él): Base (no suffix beyond the verb class)

## Different Verb Classes

The pattern varies by verb type:
- **-AR verbs** (hablar, cantar, bailar): -o, -as, -a, -amos, -áis, -an
- **-ER verbs** (comer, beber): -o, -es, -e, -emos, -éis, -en
- **-IR verbs** (vivir, escribir): -o, -es, -e, -imos, -ís, -en

## Practice Drill

Repeat daily:
- Yo hablo español
- Tú hablas muy bien
- Él habla con acento

This reinforces the conjugation pattern through context and repetition.

## Common Mistakes

- Forgetting the accent: **vosotros habláis** (not hablais)
- Confusing -ER and -AR endings
- Over-applying rules to irregular verbs (ser, ir, tener, estar)
